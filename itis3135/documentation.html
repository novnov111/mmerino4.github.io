Getting Started

Before beginning an automotive project, identify the problem or improvement you want to make. Research your vehicle and gather the correct tools.

Always confirm that replacement parts match your vehicle's year, make, model, and trim. 

Project Preparation:

1. Identify the project.
2. Research the vehicle.
3. Gather the correct tools.
4. Read the service manual.
5. Prepare the workspace.

Safety Guidelines

Safety should always come first when working on a vehicle. Automotive repairs can involve heavy equipment, electricity, chemicals, and moving parts.

  • Wear safety glasses and gloves.
  • Work on a stable, level surface.
  • Use properly rated jack stands.
  • Keep the workspace clean.
  • Follow the manufacturer's instructions.

Vehicle Inspection

Regular inspections help identify problems before they become expensive repairs. Check the vehicle's exterior and mechanical components regularly.

  1. Check for fluid leaks.
  2. Inspect the tires.
  3. Check exterior lights.
  4. Inspect visible belts and hoses.
  5. Look for unusual noises or vibrations.

Wheels And Tires

Wheels and tires affect handling, traction, and vehicle appearance. Always use the correct wheel specifications for your vehicle.

Check tire pressure regularly and follow the recommended pressure listed on the vehicle's door-jamb placard. 

Wheel Inspection:

1. Check tire pressure.
2. Inspect tread depth.
3. Look for sidewall damage.
4. Check wheel condition.
5. Follow the specified lug nut torque.

Brake System

The braking system is one of the most important safety systems on a vehicle. Regularly inspect brake pads, rotors, brake lines, and brake fluid.

If you notice grinding noises, fluid leaks, or a soft brake pedal, have the vehicle inspected before driving.

Suspension System

The suspension system helps maintain vehicle stability, ride comfort, and tire contact with the road.

Suspension modifications can change ride height and handling. After installing suspension components, inspect clearances and have the vehicle professionally aligned.

Coil springs store significant energy. Always use appropriate equipment and manufacturer-approved procedures.
